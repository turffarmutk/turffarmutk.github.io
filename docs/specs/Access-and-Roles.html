1. The model: login → your dashboard

The app opens straight to a login prompt. After sign-in, it drops you on your dashboard — a home screen built from tiles switched on or off by your role. One app, one codebase; the difference between what a student and a faculty member see is just which tiles appear. Changing someone's access is a setting, not a new version.

Beyond roles: Bill can also grant an individual person extra permissions on top of their base role — e.g. letting a specific undergrad log chemical applications or adjust inventory. So access = role defaults + per-person grants.
Undergrad lab affiliation: undergrads are usually seasonal and shared farm-wide (under Bill). But an undergrad can be assigned to a specific lab when that comes up, which scopes who can assign to and see them. So each person carries a "lab" field that can be set, changed, or left blank (= shared pool).

4. Tile rules, line by line

My tasks

Everyone sees their own tasks. Bill creates and assigns to anyone and is the only person who can assign a task to an undergrad. Technicians & grad students can create tasks and assign them to themselves. Faculty can view all tasks farm-wide, create tasks, and assign only to members of their own lab. Undergrads cannot create tasks; they check off completion on what's assigned to them.

Task checklists (confirmed): a task can carry a checklist of sub-items — e.g. the individual plots to mow, or the trial areas to paint-dot — that the worker ticks off as they go so they don't lose their place. The task completes as its items get done. See §8.

Punch in / out

Informal presence tracking (not payroll of record). Undergrad hourly + any future hourly hires clock in/out. Bill reviews the log to cross-check against official payroll and can edit or add a missed punch and annotate it. See §5.

Crew schedule (shared undergrad schedules)

Replaces the old live "who's here now" board with a forward-looking crew schedule so Bill knows who's coming in each day and can assign the day's tasks to people who'll actually be there.

How undergrads enter it: set a usual weekly pattern once (e.g. Mon/Wed/Fri), then log exceptions — extra days, changes, or time off. Entry is self-service, but every add/change notifies Bill.

Unavailability calendar: a calendar view of when undergrads are out (vacation, planned absences) so Bill sees gaps ahead of time.

Same-day call-out: a quick "I can't come in today" action (sickness, life events) that immediately pings Bill and updates the day's roster.

No-show designation (Bill only): when someone was scheduled but didn't show and never called with a reason, Bill can mark that day a no-show. Each no-show increments a running count per undergrad that Bill sees on his app — a simple accountability tally over time. (A logged call-out or excused absence is not a no-show.)

Who sees it: Bill plus technicians/grad students (crew leads) see the full schedule; undergrads see and edit their own. The no-show count is visible to Bill only. Applies to undergrad hourly + future hourly hires.

Maps (aerial / mowing / cut height)

Everyone views. Map data (cut heights, which-mower-where) is mostly stable and Bill has final say on all of it. Faculty can submit a height-change request that Bill approves/applies. See §6.

Plot restrictions

Types: No mow, No irrigate, No fungicide, No herbicide, No insecticide, No fertilizer, No wetting agent. Bill, faculty, grad students, and technicians can place restrictions on their own lab's current research plots. Every restriction requires a start and end date. A restriction can be lifted only by Bill or the person who set it. Undergrads see restrictions but cannot set them.

Equipment

A status board Bill controls (available / in use / down), plus a checkout log. Checkout is automatic when a task starts (the machine that job uses), so undergrads are recorded in the log without a manual step. Technicians & grad students can report a problem → it flags the machine and auto-creates a repair task assigned to Bill (undergrads route problems through Bill). Only Bill or a technician confirms a machine Down. Completing the repair task auto-returns it to Available. Full detail in the Equipment spec.

Field-ops logging

Technicians, grad students, and Bill log field operations; faculty view. Chemical applications (fungicide/herbicide/insecticide/fert/wetting agent) can be logged by any tech/grad/Bill, and Bill can grant a specific undergrad that ability. Completing a mowing task auto-creates a mowing log entry on those plots.

Trial protocols + study plans

Technicians, grad students, and faculty can add trial protocol info (start/end dates, target effect, associated restrictions with their own dates, etc.), and everyone can see the protocols. Planned/upcoming studies can be submitted by faculty, grad students, technicians, and Bill.

Inventory

In v1. Categories: fungicides, herbicides, insecticides, fertilizer (granular + liquid), paint (cans + liquid), seed, wetting agents. Anyone except undergrads (techs, grads, faculty, Bill) can log usage or restocks; an undergrad only if Bill grants it. QR codes on items enable scan-to-log for fast usage/restock entry.

5. Student worker dashboard (fully specified)

When an undergrad logs in, their home screen shows five tiles:

  1. My tasks — what Bill assigned them, with completion check-off and sub-item checklist.
  2. My schedule — set their usual work days, log time off, and send a same-day "can't come in" call-out.
  3. Punch in / out — clock in on arrival, out on leaving (location-gated).
  4. Maps — interactive aerial with restrictions, mowing-by-mower, and cut heights.
  5. Equipment — available vs. down, and their checkout record.

No trials, inventory, or spray records clutter their view — unless Bill has granted a specific extra ability (e.g. chemical logging or inventory), which then appears for that person only.

6. Time clock — presence tracking

Informal presence tracking, not the official payroll record. It gives Bill a clean, timestamped in/out log to cross-reference when approving payroll.

  • Who: undergrad hourly + future hourly hires (role-based, so new hourly staff get it automatically).
  • Captures: clock-in, clock-out, date, person — for Bill's payroll cross-check. (The forward-looking crew schedule is separate — see §4.)
  • Bill: reviews the roster, and can edit/add a missed punch and annotate it.
  • Not doing (v1): calculating pay, breaks, or acting as the legal timekeeping record.
Decided: clock-in works only when physically at the farm — a location check gates the clock-in button so presence reflects actual on-site time.

7. Interactive maps

Fuse your existing aerial, mowing, and height maps into one interactive map — tap a plot, get everything for that spot. Built as layers over the aerial base:

Base — Aerial plot map

Existing aerial imagery divided into the real plots/blocks and their numbering. The canvas for everything else.

Layer — Mowing by mower

Which areas get mowed with which machine; toggle a mower and its plots light up. Bill owns this data.

Layer — Cut height

Target height per plot, shown on tap and shaded across the map. Bill owns it; faculty can request changes.

Layer — Restrictions live

Active restrictions (7 types, with start/end dates) flagged right on the aerial so a restricted plot is unmistakable before anyone touches it.

The payoff: tapping plot 14 shows, in one place — "Mow at 0.5" with the triplex. RESTRICTED: no mow + no fungicide until Fri (Bowling trial)."

Build note: the interactive map is the most ambitious tile and lands in a later phase than the task board. We can ship a viewer of your existing map images first, then evolve it. Either way, the authoritative plot list + numbering is the first thing to nail down.

8. Cross-cutting mechanics (surfaced during this pass)

  • Per-person permission grants: Bill can add abilities to an individual on top of their role (undergrad chemical logging, undergrad inventory, etc.).
  • Request → approve flow: faculty height-change requests go to Bill for approval; the same pattern could serve other "ask Bill" actions.
  • QR scanning: inventory items carry QR codes for scan-to-log usage/restock. (Could extend to equipment later.)
  • Auto-logging: completing a mow task writes a mowing field-ops record automatically.
  • Immediate alerts: equipment problem reports push to Bill right away.
  • Task checklists (confirmed): tasks can hold a list of sub-items — e.g. plots to mow or trial areas to paint-dot — checked off individually so crew don't lose track; the task closes as items are done.

9. Signing in — authentication & authorization

Two separate checks, and keeping them separate is what keeps outsiders out:

  • Authentication — proving who you are.
  • Authorization — being allowed into this app, via the approved-users roster.

v1 — Magic-link email + invite-only roster

  1. Bill (admin) adds a person to the roster by their UT email — the same user record that holds their role, lab, and any per-person grants.
  2. At login the person enters their @vols.utk.edu / @utk.edu email and gets a one-time link (or 6-digit code) in that inbox. No password to manage; controlling the UT inbox is the proof of identity.
  3. The app checks the email against the roster. On the list → in, with their role. Not on the list → a "request access" screen that pings Bill; no data reached either way.
Why this is enough: receiving a login link never means being allowed in — the roster is the gate. Non-farm people simply aren't on it.

Managing the roster — add, edit & deactivate people

The roster is a Bill-only admin area and the single place people are added to the app — including new hourly undergrads. Add person captures:

  • Name and UT email — the magic-link target and match key.
  • Role — Faculty / Grad student / Technician / Undergrad hourly.
  • Lab — the lab they belong to; left blank for shared/seasonal undergrads.
  • Per-person grants — optional extra abilities (e.g. chemical logging, inventory logging).

Saving sends a magic-link invite. Role drives what they see — adding someone as Undergrad hourly automatically turns on the crew-schedule and clock-in tiles and prompts them to set their availability for the current term. Bill can edit role/lab/grants anytime, deactivate a person to lock them out immediately, and reactivate a returning seasonal worker later. This one roster feeds every module — schedule, time clock, task assignment, inventory access.

Later add-on — Institutional SSO optional · later

Institutional single sign-on (e.g. a campus NetID) can drop in later as an additional sign-in method — the roster, roles, grants, and all data stay exactly as they are; only the identity step changes. Deferring it costs nothing structurally. Its one bonus is automatic off-boarding when someone leaves the university.

Off-boarding (v1)

When someone leaves the team, Bill deactivates them on the roster and they're locked out immediately. Manual, but trivial at ~25 people.

Optional to test: "Sign in with Microsoft" using existing UT accounts might work, though UT's tenant may require admin approval — treat it as a nice-to-have, not a dependency.

10. How access is actually enforced

Hiding a tile is tidiness, not security. Real enforcement is on the server: every request is checked against the signed-in user's role and any per-person grants before data is returned or changed. So even poking at the app directly, an undergrad couldn't set a restriction, and a grad couldn't lift another lab's restriction. Ties to the Data Security section of the main plan.

11. Open questions

Resolved this pass

  • Task checklists: yes — sub-items (plots to mow, areas to paint-dot) checked off individually.
  • Clock-in location: only at the farm (location-gated).
  • Undergrad affiliation: shared/farm-wide by default; can be assigned to a lab as needed.
  • Login: v1 = magic-link email sign-in + invite-only roster. Institutional SSO can be added later — see §9.

Still to settle

  1. Plot numbering: plot names exist; still need a trial numbering system the map + protocols hang on. Deferred, but required before the maps tile.
  2. Restriction granularity: restrictions can target a whole plot or a specific area within a plot (the trial footprint), so each restriction should carry a plot plus an optional area-within-plot. Confirm how areas get drawn/defined on the map.
