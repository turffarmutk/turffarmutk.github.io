Trial Protocols & Study Plans

Where the research lives on the farm: every study's protocol — plots, dates, treatments, and the restrictions it needs — visible to all, with a forward calendar so Bill sees what's coming and gets alerted whenever a plot is restricted. Companion to the other module specs.

1. What this module does

Every research study on the farm gets a protocol — who owns it, its objective, which plots and dates, the treatments it applies, and the restrictions it needs (no-mow, no-spray, etc.). Protocols are visible to everyone, so the crew and Bill always know what's living on a plot. A forward calendar shows planned and active studies so Bill can prep, and he's alerted whenever a restriction is placed on a plot so new constraints never surprise him. v1 is protocols and planning only — no measurement/data collection.

2. Who can do what

Everyone can see every protocol — transparency is the point. Editing is scoped to the owning lab (plus Bill).

3. The study record — one entry, three stages

A study is a single record that moves through stages; no re-entering it when it starts:

StageMeans
Plannedsubmitted as an upcoming study — shows on Bill's forward calendar so he can prep
Activethe study is running — its protocol is live and its restrictions are in force on the map
Completedfinished and archived — kept for the record; its restrictions have ended

The owning lab advances the stages. No approval gate — the module is informational: Bill sees what's planned and coordinates directly rather than signing off. Restrictions carry their own start/end dates, so they activate and expire on schedule regardless of stage.

Smart entry — upload a protocol PDF

Instead of typing everything, the person can upload the study's protocol PDF. The app reads it and pre-fills what it can detect — title, objective, dates, treatments/products, and plots — leaving every field editable to fix anything misread. The PDF is also kept attached to the study as the source of record. Fields the PDF doesn't cover (like the on-map push-pin) are still set by hand. Treat extraction as best-effort, not authoritative.

5. Restrictions

Restrictions are attached to a study and are what protect the research from routine farm work. They flow straight onto the interactive map and surface to the crew through tasks (and to spray logging in inventory).

Types & scope

Seven types: No mow, No irrigate, No fungicide, No herbicide, No insecticide, No fertilizer, No wetting agent. Each targets a whole plot, a spot within a plot (the trial's pin), or a non-plot area (alleyway, border), and carries its own start and end date.

Who sets & lifts

Bill, faculty, grad students, and technicians can place restrictions on their own lab's plots. A restriction can be lifted only by Bill or the person who set it.

Bill is alerted

Whenever a restriction is placed on a plot, Bill gets an in-app alert — this is the key signal that a plot's constraints just changed, so he can plan crew work around it. (This is the module's "conflict" handling: the calendar shows everything; the alert flags new restrictions specifically.)

6. The shared calendar (studies + crew)

The study calendar and the crew-availability calendar are one calendar with a layer toggle: you switch the Studies layer and the Crew availability layer on or off independently. On its own, the Studies layer lays out planned and active studies by plot and date; turning on Crew availability overlays who's working which days, so Bill can line up labor against what the research needs in a given week.

v1 shows studies rather than auto-flagging plot overlaps — Bill eyeballs the calendar for clashes — but it does actively alert him when a restriction is placed (§5), the constraint that most affects crew scheduling. Each person sees the layers they have access to: everyone sees Studies; Crew availability is Bill + techs/grads, with undergrads seeing their own.

7. Screens (v1)

  1. Studies list — all studies by stage (Planned / Active / Completed), searchable, filter by lab or plot. Default screen.
  2. Study detail — protocol fields, plots shown on the map, treatments, the restriction list, and the stage control.
  3. New / edit study — the protocol form (faculty/grad/tech for their lab; Bill anytime).
  4. Shared calendar — planned + active studies by plot/date, with a toggle to overlay crew availability (§6).

8. Notifications

  • Restriction placed on a plot → in-app alert to Bill.
  • New planned study submitted → in-app notice to Bill (appears on his calendar).

No push in this module — consistent with the app's minimal-push approach.

10. Out of scope for v1 (comes later)

  • Data collection / measurements / ratings / results (the big v1 exclusion)
  • Statistical analysis or export of trial data
  • Approval workflow (module is informational only)
  • Automatic plot-overlap conflict detection (v1 shows the calendar; Bill eyeballs)
  • Linking treatments to inventory products (free text in v1)

11. Decisions & dependencies

Decisions:

  • Treatments: free text in v1 — no link to inventory products.
  • Stage transitions: the owning lab advances Planned → Active → Completed (no Bill sign-off); a study's restrictions auto-expire on their end date.
  • Restriction alert: in-app only to Bill (no push).

Dependencies:

  1. Interactive map: plot selection and dropping a push-pin within a plot read from the maps module — pins are far simpler than drawing footprints, but the map still needs the plot list/numbering settled (shared across modules).
  2. Protocol PDF extraction: best-effort auto-fill (title/objective/dates/treatments likely; plots/footprint less so), always editable — not authoritative.
